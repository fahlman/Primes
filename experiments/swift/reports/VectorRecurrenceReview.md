# Runtime recurrence for 128-bit factors 65–111

This is one unmerged source candidate on `swift/vector-recurrence`, based on current development `1d0522115846d8c6487d49a9bf0e60b18b9d8599`. It implements Claude's proposed runtime recurrence only in the 128-bit handler. Exact source candidate: `616d0bdc184dd91c1dd30be0c7883f2880e057ce`; [PR #25](https://github.com/fahlman/Primes/pull/25). Correctness and independent source/assembly review passed. The single registered timing session completed: **tested and rejected for adoption; unmerged**. The branch and all evidence are preserved.

The previous 24-case switch and its inline helper are replaced by one loop that visits complete 128-bit chunks in order. Each chunk contains one or two multiples, represented by separate single-bit OR operations into a local `SIMD2<UInt64>`. The branch keeps the old generated Swift tool unchanged as a historical artifact; its marked block no longer exists here, so neither `--check` nor `--write` applies to this candidate.

The replaced source region shrinks from 2,271 to 56 lines, including comments and blank lines. The complete sieve shrinks from 3,679 to 1,464 lines, a reduction of 2,215 lines. The prefix before the 128-bit handler and suffix beginning at `primes()` are byte-identical to development. Factor 3, factors 5–63, sparse factors above 111, allocation, factor discovery, enumeration and storage access are unchanged, as are the benchmark and separately compiled observer. No other refactor is included.

## Correctness argument

The unchanged dispatcher first discovers an unmarked odd factor at runtime, starting from 3. Earlier paths handle factors below 64; this path therefore receives an odd `p` in 65...111. It handles every such value uniformly and encodes no knowledge of primality. Marking starts at `(p*p-3)/2`. The unchanged scalar peel reaches a 128-bit boundary in at most 127 marks because odd `p` and 128 are coprime, or stops at the unchanged byte-rounded endpoint first.

At the aligned start, `first = 0`. At every subsequent chunk, `0 <= first < p < 128`, so the first mark exists and its lane index `first >> 6` is either 0 or 1. Since `p > 64`, at most two marks fit in 128 bits. A second mark, when present, is always in lane 1. Its source operation is `value[1] |= UInt64(1) << (first+p-64)`. The shift is always positive; Swift's ordinary `<<` returns zero when the shift is at least 64. Thus an absent second mark leaves the lane unchanged, without an invalid index or a wrapping shift that could set an unrelated bit. No source mask combines composites.

After a chunk, let `t = first+p-128`. If `t >= 0`, only the first mark fitted and `t` is the next chunk's first offset. Otherwise two marks fitted and `t+p` is the next offset. The lower bound in the second case is `2*p-128 > 0`; the upper bound follows from `t < 0`. In the first case, `t < p` because the old `first < 128`. Consequently one correction suffices to preserve `0 <= first < p`. This is the sequence `(-128*j) mod p` for chunk index `j`, with a full period of `p` chunks.

The loop requires `word < byteCount/16`. Every 16-byte raw load/store fits the allocation, and `word &* 16` equals the ordinary mathematical offset. Raw unaligned access and per-lane little-endian conversion retain the existing byte representation. Runtime state remains a fresh, dynamically sized, class-owned one-bit buffer on every pass.

Unlike the prior loop, this one may stop after any complete chunk. Its scalar tail therefore resumes at `word*128+first`, the next unmarked multiple, rather than at the next chunk's bit zero. Less than one chunk remains, containing at most two marks. The peel and tail retain `byteCount << 3` as their endpoint, including padding bits such as 1,000,001 marked by factor 101 at a one-million limit. Small-factor paths retain their distinct existing endpoints unchanged.

## Planned verification and admission

The original Verify, ExtraVerify, PhaseVerify and PhaseSieve files remain byte-identical. The additional `RecurrenceVerify.swift` checks a complete recurrence period and second-shift behavior for every odd factor 65...111. For each factor actually prime at runtime, it compares complete production buffers to historical PhaseSieve at every chunk-end residue in one period after alignment. It covers both the allocated-byte threshold `2*endBit-13` and logical endpoint `2*endBit+1`, each with offsets −1, 0 and 1. It deduplicates limits and includes one million explicitly. This targets the newly introduced arbitrary chunk exits; the historical copy supplies correctness flags only and must not profile this candidate.

Central verification will run all six original ASAN/WMO checks and this additional verifier under both configurations. ## Completed verification and independent admission

All 19 central commands exited zero on M4 Pro / Swift 6.3.3 under the exclusive lock: version, separate observer, candidate assembly, and eight verifier compile/run pairs. Verify and ExtraVerify passed under ASan and WMO, including complete lists through 10M, every limit through 30,000, 11,382 128-bit boundary limits, random/prime-square coverage and 3,654 sparse boundaries. Historical PhaseVerify passed 53,015 partial/full checks over 2,305 limits in each configuration; the candidate's full raw flags, including padding, match the reference. Its partial modes exercise the historical reference, not new partial candidate timings. The added RecurrenceVerify passed 2,112 recurrence/shift states and 1,261 arbitrary-chunk raw-buffer comparisons under both configurations. These are repeated configurations of the same cases, not doubled distinct inputs.

Independent Codex source review confirmed unchanged source outside the 128-bit handler/helper and unchanged supporting references, as well as recurrence, load/store bounds, padding and tail correctness. The installed Swift interface's nonmasking-shift implementation confirms that positive shifts of at least the word width produce zero; the focused verifier exercised that behavior. Generator --check is intentionally inapplicable to the removed block; the legacy tool remains untouched as historical source. No verifier was weakened or replaced.

Independent emitted-code review found no blocker. The old outlined 8,061-instruction vector helper disappears. runSieve grows from 907 to 995 instructions and its frame from 240 to 288 bytes. The new recurrence loop is 23 instructions including its backedge, with four stack-temporary accesses (vector store/reload and selected-lane scalar load/store). Its optional-second correction uses cmp/csel; the next-offset correction uses a sign mask and add. Neither requires a data-dependent branch. The 2,530-instruction 64-bit helper remains normalized unchanged. Four sparse loops retain 51 instructions, 16 stores and no hot-loop stack access, though register assignments change. The 30-instruction completedPass path retains initialization, sieving, the real separately compiled observer and release; initializer/destructor are unchanged. This is not an assembly-identity or performance claim.

The cached control assembly was reused only after source, frozen harness, compiler and complete assembly hashes matched its immutable record at 8bb522f. The timed control was freshly compiled alongside the candidate. Exact commands, outputs, hashes, source review, complete candidate assembly, reviewed orchestration and admission are preserved in `../vector-recurrence-evidence/`. The initial build wait respected Claude's PR #24 lock; our verification acquired and released its own lock after that run finished. Inherited all-language CI runs 34724577479 and 34724558058 were cancelled because they do not exercise experiments/swift; dedicated checks above ran.

The comparison records one compile duration per variant using a transparent compiler launcher that forwards all arguments unchanged. These are individual build observations, not a replicated compile-time study. No timing is repeated to seek a win. The single registered session ran 2026-09-12 23:14:08–23:15:35 UTC on Apple M4 Pro / Swift 6.3.3. It fails the registered admission rule and is rejected for adoption. Current development remains unchanged; the explicit 65–111 handlers are retained. No measured trial or session was discarded or repeated. The conditional actual-submission SwiftPM and native Linux checks were not reached, and no claim is made for those contexts.

These are single-build observations, affected by machine conditions, cache state and build order; they do not establish a replicated compile-time distribution. The shorter build did not meet the user's runtime-speed priority.

Conditions were sampled before compilation, after all trials while holding the lock, and after release. No monitored competing build/test/benchmark or audio assertion appeared. This is a development comparison on the M4 only, not an upstream-baseline or Threadripper measurement.
